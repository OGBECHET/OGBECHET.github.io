About

Hi, I'm Theresa.

I'm a PhD candidate in Economics at Southern Illinois University Carbondale, where I also earned my master's degree. I work as an applied economist on energy, the environment, and development.

My research starts from something I lived with growing up in Nigeria: being connected to the grid doesn't mean the lights are on. A lot of research treats electricity as a yes-or-no question. I'm more interested in reliability, and in what an unreliable supply means for a woman giving birth at night or a clinic trying to keep vaccines cold. My dissertation follows that question across ten Sub-Saharan African countries, using household surveys, health facility data, and satellite measures of nighttime light.

I came to economics by way of geography. My background covers GIS, environmental resources, and hydrography, and before moving to the United States I worked as research staff with the Nigerian Navy. That training shapes how I work now: I think spatially, and I like data that has a location attached to it.

Outside my own research, I've worked on corporate decarbonization through EDF Climate Corps and the Purdue Climate Corps. I also care about getting more people from underrepresented backgrounds into science.
